## Your deadline to appeal
If your employer plan denies a claim, the plan must give you at least 180 days after you get the denial notice to file an internal appeal (29 CFR 2560.503-1(h)(3)(i)). Count from the date on your notice to be safe.

## What a full and fair review includes
On appeal you may send written comments, documents and records. You can ask the plan, free of charge, for copies of the documents and records relevant to your claim. The review must take into account everything you submit (29 CFR 2560.503-1(h)(2)).

## Medical judgment on appeal
When a denial is based on medical judgment, such as whether a treatment is medically necessary or experimental, the plan must consult a health care professional with appropriate training and experience in that field during the appeal (29 CFR 2560.503-1(h)(3)(iii)).

## Urgent care appeals
For urgent care claims, the plan must decide the appeal as soon as possible given the medical circumstances, and no later than 72 hours after it gets your request (29 CFR 2560.503-1(i)(2)).

## External review
After a final internal denial, most non-grandfathered plans must let you ask for an independent external review. Under the federal process the request must be filed within four months after you receive the final internal denial notice (45 CFR 147.136(d)(2)). An external reviewer's decision binds the plan.
